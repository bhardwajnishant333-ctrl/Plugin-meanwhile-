## Turn 2 — Meanwhile
**Nobody recorded the temperature. The weight is documented, and it's horrifying: a lump of lead of about 7 ounces (200 g), cut out of a 94-year-old man's stomach.**

On the night of 1–2 December 1755, Rudyerd's mostly timber Eddystone tower caught fire. The keeper, Henry Hall, 94, was throwing water up at the burning lantern when molten lead dripped down; he said it went into his open mouth. He lived about 12 days. Plymouth physician Edward Spry did the post-mortem and reported a flat, oval piece of lead in the stomach — usually quoted as **7 oz 5 drachms**. Spry published with the Royal Society in 1756; when doubted, he fed molten lead to animals to show it could be survived for a while. Confident on the account, not on the second decimal of the weight.

**Temperature:** no exact figure exists, and anyone who gives you one is making it up. Physics says only that it was **at least 327.5 °C (621.5 °F)**, lead's melting point.
