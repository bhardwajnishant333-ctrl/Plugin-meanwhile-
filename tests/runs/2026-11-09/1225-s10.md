The first machine to slice bread was built by a jeweller, and it was dismissed because sliced bread "goes stale."

Otto Rohwedder was a jeweller from Iowa. He started working on a bread slicer around 1912, and a fire in 1917 destroyed his prototype and plans. Bakeries turned him down because a sliced loaf dries out within hours, and he reportedly tried holding the slices together with hatpins. He solved the problem by adding a machine that wrapped the loaf in waxed paper right after slicing. The first sliced bread was sold on 7 July 1928 by the Chillicothe Baking Company in Missouri. Within about five years, most bread sold in the US came sliced. The exact share is often quoted around 80%, but I'd treat that as approximate.

The twist: the slicer alone didn't work. It took slicing and wrapping together to fix staleness. Inventions often stall until somebody solves the unglamorous problem right beside them.
