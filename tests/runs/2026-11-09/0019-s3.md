Erosion was first seen after an early shuttle flight in 1981. The engineers studied it, decided there was still a safety margin, and the flight was treated as a success. Each later flight that came back with erosion and no failure became evidence that erosion was acceptable. The coldest launch before Challenger, in January 1985 at about 53°F, showed the worst hot-gas blow-by so far.

On the night before 28 January 1986, Thiokol engineers including Roger Boisjoly and Allan McDonald argued against launching below 53°F. The forecast was in the 20s and 30s °F. At every step people followed procedure and pointed to data. The acceptable range had simply grown, one flight at a time.
