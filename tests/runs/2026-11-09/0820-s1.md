**Meanwhile:** **CAN has no bus master, yet a broken node reliably switches itself off. It does this with two counters and lopsided scoring.**

Each node keeps a transmit error counter (TEC) and a receive error counter (REC). A failed transmit adds **8** to TEC; a successful one subtracts **1**. A receive error adds 1 to REC. The node's state follows from these:
- **Error-active** (both counters ≤127): when it sees an error, it sends **6 dominant bits**. That deliberately breaks the 5-bit stuffing rule, so every node on the bus sees a stuff error and the frame is thrown away everywhere at once.
- **Error-passive** (either counter >127): its error flag is **6 recessive bits**, which can't override anyone's traffic. It can still report trouble but can no longer destroy other nodes' frames. It also has to wait 8 extra bit times before it transmits again.
- **Bus-off** (TEC >255): it stops transmitting completely. It may rejoin only after seeing 128 runs of 11 recessive bits.

Why this lands on the right node: the faulty node keeps failing its own transmits and gains 8 each time. Healthy nodes only see those failures as receivers and gain 1. So the scoring is lopsided against whoever is causing the trouble, with no central coordinator making the call. With +8 against −1, a node that fails more than about 1 transmit in 9 drifts toward bus-off.

**The twist:** the mechanism can be turned into an attack. Cho and Shin (ACM CCS 2016) showed that an attacker on the bus can flip bits only in the victim's frames, pushing the victim's TEC up 8 at a time until it shuts itself off. The attack takes about a dozen frames and leaves nothing that looks like an attack. The feature that removes bad nodes becomes a way to remove good ones.

**Why it matters for your port:** on the STM32G4's FDCAN (Bosch M_CAN), I believe bus-off sets `CCCR.INIT`, and the controller stays silent until software clears it. On a motor controller, that means it quietly stops receiving torque commands. Check whether the Embassy FDCAN driver recovers automatically, surfaces bus-off as an error, or just waits forever. I'm not sure which it does today.
