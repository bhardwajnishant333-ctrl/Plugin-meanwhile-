Swing isn't the 2:1 triplet feel your sheet music writes down. Measured drummers swing as wide as about 3.5:1 at slow tempos, and close to 1:1 (straight eighths) at fast ones.

**Mechanism:** Anders Friberg and Andreas Sundström timed ride-cymbal patterns on recordings by well-known jazz drummers ("Swing ratios and ensemble timing in jazz performance," *Music Perception*, 2002). The long-short ratio isn't a constant. It shrinks steadily as tempo rises, because the short note stays at a roughly fixed length, somewhere near 100 ms, while the beat gets shorter around it. At slow ballad tempos the ratio goes well past a triplet. At burning tempos it flattens almost to even. The triplet figure is just roughly where medium tempos land. Treat my exact millisecond figure as approximate. The direction of the effect is the solid finding.

**Twist:** In the same paper, soloists played *behind* the drummer on the downbeats but were *in sync* on the offbeats. So the "laid-back" feel isn't a uniform delay. The soloist is late on 1 and 3 and lands exactly on the "and." The written notation captures neither part of this: it shows one fixed ratio and assumes everyone is perfectly together. Practising with a metronome on 2 and 4 is closer to how swing actually works than any ratio you could count out.

**Meanwhile:**
Classic cron already handles the night the clock falls back. The DST bug that has actually been reported for Celery beat is a different one: on the day the clock changes, its jobs fire an hour off.

**Mechanism:** Vixie cron and cronie handle DST explicitly. If the clock goes back by less than 3 hours, a job pinned to a fixed time in the repeated hour (your 1:30 a.m.) does *not* run a second time. If the clock jumps forward, jobs that fell in the skipped hour run right after the jump. Jobs with a `*` in the hour or minute field just follow the new wall clock. That's all written down in `man 8 cron`.

django-celery-beat issue #134, "Period Tasks Don't Adjust for Daylight Savings", was opened on 15 March 2018. A `0 14 * * 4` task with `TIME_ZONE = America/Los_Angeles` fired at 3 p.m. instead of 2 p.m., as if DST hadn't started, and then fixed itself the next week. The issue was closed in December 2018, reopened in March 2022, and people kept adding the same report: one daily job set for 16:04 New York time ran at 17:04 on 9 March. Every report describes the same pattern: one firing on the DST day is an hour off, then it's back to normal. I couldn't find a confirmed report of beat running the repeated-hour job twice, and I haven't traced the root cause in the code, so I won't guess at it.

**Twist:** For reminder emails, an email arriving an hour early or late once a year is harmless. The real risk is in the query. If the task picks recipients with something like `appointment__range=(now, now + timedelta(hours=24))`, then on a 23-hour or 25-hour day that window either skips an hour of appointments or covers one twice. Two habits protect you whatever beat does:
- Select by calendar date in the clinic's own timezone (`localdate() + 1 day`), not by "now plus 24 hours".
- Make each send idempotent with a `reminder_sent_at` field, so a duplicate or shifted firing can't email the same owner twice.

Assuming you're in the US, DST ended on 1 November, so the next change your migration will hit is 14 March 2027.

Your test is trying to reach a real Redis broker. `.delay()` is `apply_async()`, and `apply_async()` publishes the task to the broker at `localhost:6379`. Nothing is listening there in your test environment, so kombu fails with "connection refused". The task code itself never ran, so this says nothing about the reminder logic.

**Fix (best for a unit test):** call the task as a plain function, so it runs right there in the test process:

```python
# appointments/tests/test_tasks.py, line 41
send_appointment_reminders()          # was: send_appointment_reminders.delay()
```

If the task is `bind=True`, use `send_appointment_reminders.apply()` instead. It runs locally with a real task context and never touches the broker. Then assert on `mail.outbox` the same way you do now.

**If other tests or code paths call `.delay()`** (for example a view that queues the task), turn on eager mode in your **test settings file**, assuming the usual `CELERY_` namespace in `config_from_object`:

```python
CELERY_TASK_ALWAYS_EAGER = True
CELERY_TASK_EAGER_PROPAGATES = True   # exceptions inside the task fail the test instead of being swallowed
```

Put these in the settings module the test run loads, not in `@override_settings`. Celery reads its config once, so a per-test override often doesn't reach it.

**Why the old suite didn't hit this:** under cron the reminders presumably ran through a management command, called directly. The migration added a `.delay()` call to the test. When your agent restarts, tell it to apply the direct-call fix and rerun the suite. Don't add Redis to CI just for this.
