Great frigatebirds can stay airborne for weeks over the open ocean, and they get by on about 42 minutes of sleep a day.

**How it works:** In 2016, Niels Rattenborg's team fitted EEG loggers to great frigatebirds nesting in the Galápagos and recorded their brain activity on flights lasting up to about 10 days. In the air the birds slept in bursts of seconds, averaging around 12 seconds, mostly while gliding up on rising columns of warm air. Sometimes only one half of the brain slept, and it was usually the side that kept the forward-facing eye open in the direction of flight, so the bird could watch where it was going. Most ringed birds are never heard from again. For small songbirds, often fewer than 1 in 100 rings are ever reported back. The few that do come back show where people found the birds, more than where the birds went.

**How it works:** A recovery takes three things: the bird dies or is caught, a person finds it, and that person reports the ring. Each step is biased toward places with people. Ringing maps for Arctic terns had a dense cluster on the coasts of Ghana and West Africa. That wasn't because terns mostly wintered there. It was because boys there traditionally trapped terns on the beaches, and the rings got reported. Ghanaian conservation groups worked with UK partners from the 1980s onward to end the practice. Meanwhile, the Southern Ocean, where many of the terns actually spend the winter, produced almost no recoveries, because almost nobody is there to pick up a dead tern from the pack ice. Roads, beaches, hunting seasons and countries with a habit of reporting rings all add to the bias.

**The twist:** For decades, our picture of migration was partly a map of where people live, until geolocators showed the long routes over open ocean that nobody had seen. It's the same survivorship bias as the WWII bullet-hole planes, and it's in your field data too. Bug reports come from devices whose owners noticed, cared and had a phone nearby. The firmware that silently drops a connection in a field with no signal never files a report.
