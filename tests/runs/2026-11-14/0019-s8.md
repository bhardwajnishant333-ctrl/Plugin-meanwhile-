**Meanwhile:**
FT8 is famous for decoding signals 20 dB *below* the noise. That's mostly a bookkeeping trick.

**How it works:** WSJT-X reports SNR in a 2500 Hz reference bandwidth. FT8 uses 8 tones spaced 6.25 Hz apart; at any instant all its power sits in one 6.25 Hz tone. Narrowing from 2500 Hz to 6.25 Hz cuts noise by 10·log₁₀(400) ≈ 26 dB, so −21 dB at threshold is about +5 dB in the decoder's bandwidth. 77 message bits are wrapped in a (174,91) LDPC code and sent as 79 symbols over 12.64 s, with precise timing.

**The twist:** You never beat the noise. You stop listening to most of it, and you pay with time — Shannon's bandwidth/power/time trade, audible. Moonbounce modes push the same lever further over a ~250 dB path.

**Meanwhile:**
On 2 m moonbounce, A can hear B perfectly while B hears nothing from A, using the same antennas, the same Moon and the same moment.

Two effects twist linear polarization: spatial offset (geometry, up to 90°) and Faraday rotation (ionosphere, ∝ 1/f², 81× weaker at 1296 MHz than 144 MHz).

**The twist:** Faraday rotation is non-reciprocal; the spatial offset flips sign when you swap who transmits. So one path can cancel while the other adds to ~90°: "one-way propagation". Hence crossed Yagis + MAP65 on 2 m, and circular polarization at 23 cm and up (Moon reflection flips the sense).

1) **Deeper:** dual-polarization receive in software
2) **Sideways:** libration fading
3) **Wildcard:** the Faraday isolator in every fiber laser

**Meanwhile:**
A Faraday isolator is a one-way door for light. The physics underneath is the Moon-path problem from the last round.

Polarizer at 0° → TGG Faraday rotator in a magnet (45°) → polarizer at 45°. Back-reflections get rotated another 45° in the same sense, arrive at 90° and are blocked; 30–40 dB per stage. DFB telecom lasers often ship with one built in.

**The twist:** Lorentz reciprocity means no ordinary linear optic can do this; the magnet breaks time-reversal symmetry. That's why on-chip isolators are hard in silicon photonics. Same principle as the ferrite circulators on 23 cm EME stations.

1) **Deeper:** magnet-free isolators in silicon photonics
