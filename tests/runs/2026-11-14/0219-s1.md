**A honeybee swarm picks its new home with a quorum, and it never needs everyone to agree.**

When a colony swarms, a few hundred scouts out of roughly 10,000 bees search for cavities and waggle-dance for the ones they like. Better sites get longer, more enthusiastic dances, so more scouts go and check them out. Thomas Seeley (Cornell, *Honeybee Democracy*, 2010) showed the swarm doesn't wait for the dances to agree. Each scout counts how many other scouts she runs into at her site. Once that hits a threshold (his experiments put it around 15 to 30 bees present at once), the scouts switch to "piping." That signal tells the cluster to warm its flight muscles for takeoff, and the swarm launches within about an hour.

**The twist:** in 2012 Seeley's team found a second mechanism. Scouts for one site head-butt dancers for rival sites and give a short buzz, a "stop signal" that shuts that dancer up. This is cross-inhibition, and it stops the swarm from splitting its vote between two equally good sites forever, which is the classic failure mode of distributed consensus. Raft and Paxos also commit on a quorum instead of unanimity. The bees just found the deadlock-breaker about 30 million years earlier. The quorum threshold works as a speed-versus-accuracy dial: a low quorum gives a fast, riskier choice, and a high quorum gives a slow, safer one. Swarms reportedly lower it when the weather turns.

**It only hurts if the calendar stores future classes as UTC. And "always store UTC" is the advice most of us were given.**

Here's how it goes wrong. A recurring 7:00am class saved as UTC locks in *today's* time-zone rules for that date, and governments change those rules, sometimes with almost no warning. In 2016 Egypt cancelled daylight saving only days before it was due to start. In October 2022 Mexico abolished most of its DST, days before the autumn change. Every app that had already converted future local times into UTC was now off by an hour, and nothing in the database looked wrong. Jon Skeet wrote this up as "Storing UTC is not a silver bullet" (2019).

**The twist:** UTC is the right truth for things that *already happened*, like a payment timestamp or when someone booked. For things that *will* happen, the truth is what the human agreed to: "7:00am, studio time." So the safe shape for future events is:

- the local date and time (`2027-03-14 07:00`)
- an IANA zone ID (`Europe/London`, never a fixed offset like `+01:00`)
- the UTC instant worked out when you read it, or cached and recomputed whenever tzdata updates. PHP ships its own copy of the time-zone database, so keep PHP updated.

The other trap for a studio calendar is DST transition days. On spring-forward day, 2:30am doesn't exist. On fall-back day, 1:30am happens twice. Early classes are usually safe, but a recurrence rule expanded in UTC rather than local time will push every class an hour off after the change.
