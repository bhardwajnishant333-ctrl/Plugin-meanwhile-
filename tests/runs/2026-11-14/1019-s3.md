**In the early 2000s, the US had to reinvent a material for its own nuclear warheads, because nobody remembered how it had been made.**

The material was code-named Fogbank. It's a classified component of the W76 warhead, and public reporting suggests a foam-like material inside the warhead. It was made at the Y-12 plant in Oak Ridge from about 1975 to 1989, and then production stopped. Around 2000, the W76 life-extension program needed more. The production building had been closed, most of the people who had worked there were gone, and the records were thin. The NNSA spent years and tens of millions of dollars rebuilding the process, and the program slipped by about a year.

The new batches came out wrong. Eventually the team found out why: the original product contained an impurity that the old process had introduced without anyone knowing, and the material depended on it. The modern, purer ingredients were too clean. Nobody had written the impurity down, because nobody knew it was there.

**The twist:** the original records may have been accurate and still incomplete. The real recipe included something the old system did by accident. When you rebuild something from documentation, you copy what people knew about it, which isn't always everything it did.

A modest version of this applies to you. If your PG13 cluster runs on an older OS and the PG17 images use a newer glibc (2.28 or later), text can sort differently. Logical replication rebuilds the indexes, so the data is safe. But anything in your app that quietly depends on `ORDER BY` on text could return results in a different order. It's worth spot-checking.
