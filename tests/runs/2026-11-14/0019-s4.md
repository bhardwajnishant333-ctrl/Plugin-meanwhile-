**The 3¢ "Yes" on a long-dated prediction market often isn't anyone's belief. It's the interest rate.**

Say a market resolves in 12 months on something that is almost certainly not going to happen. "No" trades at 97¢. If you buy No you tie up 97¢ for a year to make 3¢, which is about 3.1%. Treasury bills paid roughly 4-5% for most of 2024-25, and they carry no resolution risk, no platform risk and no oracle-dispute risk. So the traders who would push that price to 99.5¢ don't bother. Nobody has to believe in the 3% for the price to stay there.

It's just arithmetic: **price ≈ probability only when holding money costs nothing.** If the payout is a year away, price ≈ probability ÷ (1 + rate), plus whatever premium people want for locking up capital. Short-dated markets barely feel this. Markets on events years out can be off by several points in a predictable direction.

Kalshi seems to have noticed. In 2024 it started paying interest on cash and open positions; I think it was around 4% APY at launch, but check that number. I'm not sure whether Polymarket does the same on all its markets.

**The twist:** when someone says "the market gives it a 5% chance" about something two years out, the honest reading is "somewhere between about 0% and 5%, and most of that might be the interest rate."

**Diffusion models run the second law of thermodynamics in reverse. The math that lets them do it was published in 1982, before anyone used it to generate images.**

Sohl-Dickstein et al. (ICML 2015) took the idea from statistical physics. In 1982 Brian Anderson showed that a diffusion process has a reverse-time version needing only the score, ∇ₓ log p(x). DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) made it work at scale; Song et al. (2021) unified DDPM and score matching as one reverse-time SDE.

Where the analogy breaks: the network acts like a trained Maxwell's demon (the "reversal" is paid for in training compute); and DDIM, the probability-flow ODE and flow matching (2022) make the thermodynamics optional.

**The twist, and it applies to your run:** noise level depends on resolution. Hoogeboom et al. ("simple diffusion", 2023) and Ting Chen (2023) found schedules tuned on small images under-noise large ones. Satellite tiles have large smooth correlated regions, so if layout is incoherent, look at the schedule shift before adding capacity.

First a correction, because I oversold the teaser. The colours in satellite imagery come from military history, not politics. The part that really is political is **resolution**. Both end up in your training data.

**Colour: a WWII camouflage test.** Kodak's infrared colour film ("camouflage detection film") showed near-IR as red, so living vegetation looked bright red and painted decoys dull. That colour-infrared convention carried into Landsat-era false-colour composites.

**Resolution: a law.** The Kyl-Bingaman Amendment (1997 NDAA) capped US-licensed commercial imagery of Israel, in practice at about 2 m per pixel. In July 2020 NOAA relaxed the limit to 0.4 m.

**The twist for your run:** a tile is the ground after band choices, pansharpening, colour balancing, multi-season mosaicking and sometimes a legal cap; your model learns all of it as geography.
