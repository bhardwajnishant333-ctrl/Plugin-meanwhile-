**On 2 July 2019, one regex knocked Cloudflare off the internet for 27 minutes.**

A new WAF rule went out to every server at once. Inside it was `.*(?:.*=.*)`. A backtracking engine handles that by trying every way to split the input among the three `.*`, so when there's no match the work grows roughly with the cube of the input length. Every core handling HTTP traffic hit 100% CPU, and sites all over the world returned 502s. One fix in the postmortem was to move to RE2 or Rust's `regex` crate. Both are built on finite automata, after Ken Thompson's 1968 construction, and guarantee matching time linear in the input.

**The twist for you:** Rust's `regex` gets that guarantee by leaving out backreferences and lookaround. If your agent's "engine swap" adds `fancy-regex` or PCRE2 to get those features, the guarantee is gone. As I understand it, `fancy-regex` only falls back to backtracking when a pattern actually uses a fancy feature. And nginx logs are full of text strangers choose: paths, user agents, referers.

1) Deeper: why `.*.*=.*` explodes, step by step
2) Sideways: Stack Overflow's 2016 outage, caused by 20,000 spaces

**A robin's compass seems to need light, and AM-radio-band noise can scramble it.**

In 1972 Wolfgang and Roswitha Wiltschko showed that European robins have an *inclination* compass. They don't sense north versus south. They sense the angle the field lines make with the ground, so they know "toward the pole" versus "toward the equator."

The leading explanation is the radical-pair mechanism, which Klaus Schulten proposed in 1978. Blue light hits a protein in the retina, cryptochrome, and creates two radicals whose electron spins are correlated. Earth's field, about 50 µT, nudges how those spins evolve, and that shifts the chemical product. In 2021 a team showed robin cryptochrome 4 is magnetically sensitive in the lab. It hasn't been proven inside a living bird. That's still open.

**The twist:** in 2014 Engels and colleagues found that robins in Oldenburg lost their bearing to weak electromagnetic noise in the roughly 2 kHz–5 MHz range. Shielding their huts brought it back. Robins in cities may be partly compass-blind.

(This is the European robin, not the American one, which is a thrush.)

1) Deeper: how a signal far weaker than heat noise survives
2) Sideways: pigeons, which seem to navigate partly by smell
