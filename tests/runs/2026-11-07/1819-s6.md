**In 2011, neutrinos beat light by 60 nanoseconds. The culprit was a loose cable.**

The OPERA experiment fired neutrinos from CERN to the Gran Sasso lab in Italy, about 730 km through solid rock. They kept arriving about **60 ns early**, a result at roughly 6 sigma. If it were real, it would break special relativity. The team didn't claim a discovery. In September 2011 they published and asked other physicists to find their mistake. In February 2012 the team found it. A fibre-optic cable carrying the GPS time signal into the master clock's computer card wasn't fully plugged in. That added a delay of about 70 ns in exactly the wrong direction. (A second fault, a clock oscillator running slightly fast, pushed the other way by a smaller amount.) **The twist:** the physics was fine. What broke was the timestamp. Both events were real, but the clock that labelled one of them was off by a few dozen nanoseconds, and that was enough to "overturn Einstein." Every surprising result in a pipeline deserves the same first question: is this something that happened, or a shift in how we stamped it?
