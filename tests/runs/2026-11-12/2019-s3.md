**How it works:** The idea comes from a story in St. Olaf's saga. On a snowy, overcast day, Olaf asks Sigurd where the sun is, then checks his answer against a *sólarsteinn*, a "sunstone." The best candidate for that stone is Iceland spar, a clear calcite crystal that splits light into two images. Skylight is polarized, most strongly at 90° from the sun. Look at the sky through the crystal and the two images have different brightness. Rotate it until they match, and the angle tells you where the sun is, even through patchy cloud or after sunset. **Meanwhile:**
Logical replication copies every row but not the counters that number them. After cutover, your new primary's sequences may still be at 1.

**How it works:** Postgres logical replication decodes row changes from the WAL. A `nextval()` is not a row change, so sequence state never travels, and that's still true on PG 17 (I believe sync support is only now landing in newer versions). If the schema was created on the CNPG side from a schema-only dump, every `serial`/`identity` sequence there restarts at its START value, because the `setval()` lines are in the dump's *data* section. The rows arrive fine, `id` 1 to 4,000,000 included. Then the first INSERT after cutover calls `nextval()`, gets 1, and fails with `duplicate key value violates unique constraint`. The fix is to run `setval()` on each sequence on the new primary before writes open: either `max(id)` from the table or the old side's `last_value` plus a safety margin. The CNPG kubectl plugin has a `subscription sync-sequences` command for this, with an offset flag, if I'm remembering it right. Check `kubectl cnpg subscription --help`.

**The twist:** Cutover looks healthy. Replication lag is zero, row counts match, and read-only smoke tests pass. Nothing breaks until the first write to each table, and for you that's probably someone tapping "unlock" on a bike. The failure also arrives table by table, as each sees its first insert, so it looks like flaky app errors rather than a migration bug. One quick check before cutover: compare `last_value` from `pg_sequences` on both sides.
