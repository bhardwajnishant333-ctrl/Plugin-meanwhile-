**Your retry logic can fail in opposite ways depending on the broker: duplicate runs on Redis, a closed connection on RabbitMQ.**

When a Celery task retries with a `countdown`, the worker doesn't put it to sleep somewhere central. It takes the message off the queue right away and holds it in memory, unacknowledged, until it's due.

- **On Redis:** an unacknowledged message only stays reserved for `visibility_timeout`, which defaults to 1 hour. If a countdown runs past that, Redis assumes the worker died and hands the message out again. The same task then runs twice.
- **On RabbitMQ:** the broker has a `consumer_timeout`, which defaults to 30 minutes in modern versions (it arrived in the 3.8 series). If a delivery goes unacknowledged longer than that, RabbitMQ closes the channel with `PRECONDITION_FAILED`. The worker drops its messages, they go back on the queue, and you get a reconnect loop instead of duplicates.

**The twist:** the migration doesn't just change where the bug shows up. It changes how it fails, from silent duplicates to a loud crash. Backoff schedules are usually tuned by feel, and something like 5 min, 15, 45, 2 h sails past the 30-minute limit on the third retry. That bug ships to prod, not to tests, because nobody waits 30 minutes in a test. When Claude finishes, check the longest retry delay in the new code. I'm sure of the general mechanism, but check the exact defaults against your RabbitMQ version and your Celery `acks_late` settings.

**The two main microbes in a classic sourdough starter don't compete for food. One lives on what the other leaves behind.**

The bacterium behind San Francisco sourdough, *Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis* (formerly *Lactobacillus*), feeds mainly on maltose, the sugar flour enzymes free up from starch. It splits the maltose, uses half, and lets glucose leak out. The yeast it usually lives with, *Kazachstania humilis* (formerly *Candida milleri*), can't use maltose at all, so it lives on that leftover glucose. The bacteria also make the starter acidic enough to keep most other microbes out, and this yeast tolerates the acid. USDA researchers in San Francisco (Kline and Sugihara) described this pairing around 1970, and it's why some starters stay stable for decades.

**The twist:** a sluggish starter usually isn't dying. The partnership is out of balance, and two things tip it:

- **Temperature picks the winner.** The bacteria do best around 30°C and the yeast prefers it cooler, roughly mid-20s. A cold kitchen slows both, but not by the same amount, so the rhythm drifts. I'm confident about the direction; the exact optimum temperatures vary by strain.
- **Big feeds dilute the acid.** A 1:10:10 feed raises the pH enough that the acid barrier drops for a while. Slow strains then lose ground, and the starter looks weak right after every feed.

The usual fix follows from that: keep it warm (about 26–28°C), feed at a tighter ratio like 1:2:2 for a few days, and add some whole-grain rye for extra sugars and nutrients.

**What the traceback is really saying:** the failure isn't coming from `self.retry`. It's the original exception from line 37 (`carrier_client.push`). `retry()` re-raises that exception in two cases: when the task is called directly as a plain function (common in tests), or when it has run out of retries. So `carrier_client.push` is what's opening a kombu/AMQP connection, and that connection is being refused.

**Why "the container is up" doesn't settle it:** `Errno 111` means nothing accepted a TCP connection at the host and port the code tried. The usual causes, most likely first:

1. **Wrong host.** Your code runs inside a container (`/app/...`), so `localhost` / `127.0.0.1` points at the app container itself, not at RabbitMQ. The URL has to use the compose service name, e.g. `amqp://guest:guest@rabbitmq:5672//`.
2. **A leftover URL.** `carrier_client` may build its own connection from a different setting or env var than `CELERY_BROKER_URL`, and the migration may not have updated it. It could still point at the old Redis URL, or at port 15672 (the management UI) instead of 5672.
3. **RabbitMQ isn't ready yet.** A container that's "up" can still be booting, which often takes 5–15 seconds. A test run that starts right away gets refused.
