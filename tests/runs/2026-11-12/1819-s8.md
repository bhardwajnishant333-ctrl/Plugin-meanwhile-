The six-wheeler was mad, but it won: Scheckter and Depailler finished 1-2 at the 1976 Swedish GP. What finished it off wasn't the physics. It was the tyre supplier.

Derek Gardner's thinking behind the Tyrrell P34 made sense. Normal front tyres stuck out into the airflow and caused a lot of drag. Shrink them to tiny 10-inch wheels and they hide behind the front wing. Fit four of them so the car still has enough rubber on the road for grip and braking. In 1976 the car scored a win and a stack of podiums. Then it went downhill in 1977. Goodyear was the only supplier of those tiny front tyres, and as I understand it, the company put its development effort into the normal sizes everyone else used, partly because Michelin had just arrived and started a tyre war. The P34's front tyres stopped improving while every other team's tyres kept getting better. Tyrrell went back to four wheels for 1978. Others kept experimenting: March ran a version with four driven rear wheels, and Williams tested one in 1982 (the FW08B) that reportedly went very well. The rules then limited cars to four wheels from 1983, so we never found out.
