**The power you can hold over time drops in two stages: one is a battery, the other is a fuel tank.**

**Stage 1, minutes to about an hour: a battery.** In 1965 Monod and Scherrer described the **critical power** model: `P = CP + W'/t`.
- **CP** is the highest power you can hold for a long time without exhausting yourself.
- **W'** ("W prime") is a fixed amount of energy you can spend *above* CP. For a trained cyclist it is typically around 15-25 kJ.
- Go above CP and you drain W'. Drop below CP and it slowly refills. This is why a breakaway rider can survive several attacks and then suddenly crack: his W' hits zero.

**Stage 2, beyond about 1-2 hours: the battery model stops working.** It predicts you could ride at CP forever, and you can't. The share of your capacity you can hold keeps falling, roughly on a logarithmic curve:
- Muscle glycogen, the stored carbohydrate muscles burn hardest, lasts only around 90-120 minutes at hard effort, so after that you lean more on fat, which burns more slowly.
- Fatigue builds up in the muscles, the nervous system, and from heat.

For Garin's 18 hours, that means a fraction of his one-hour power. I'd guess about half or a little more, but that's a guess, not a measured figure.

**Twist for your game:** the W' model is close to a ready-made **boost/nitro system**, and it is better than the usual linear one:
- There is a sustainable line (CP). Going over it costs reserve, and staying under it earns reserve back.
- In Skiba's 2012 "W'bal" version, recharge is **exponential, and faster the further below CP you go**. Coasting refills fast, while running just under the line barely refills at all.
- In C#, each frame:
  - over CP: `reserve -= (P - CP) * dt`
  - under CP: `reserve += (W' - reserve) * (1 - exp(-dt / tau))`
  - make `tau` shrink as `CP - P` grows, so the recharge speeds up the further below CP you are.
- What players get: "go hard now or save it for the last corner" becomes a real decision, exactly like a breakaway rider deciding when to attack.

(Skiba published a specific formula for tau. I don't trust my memory of its constants, so tune tau by feel rather than copying a number.)
