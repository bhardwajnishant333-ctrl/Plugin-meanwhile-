**A 2001 lab study found that chalk *lowered* finger friction.**

Li, Margetts and Fowler (*Journal of Sports Sciences*, 2001, 15 climbers) found finger friction on rock fell with chalk: chalk dries and stiffens skin so it moulds less into the rock, and loose chalk acts like ball bearings. Cold "sending temps": skin friction (my understanding, not checked against a specific paper) peaks at middle moisture; shoe rubber is viscoelastic and Grosch (1963) showed its friction depends on temperature and sliding speed together, so "cold rubber grips better" isn't a simple rule.

Twist: Clarke et al. (2024) tested sandstone, granite and two limestones: chalk helped most on dry fingers, and its most reliable effect was making friction more consistent, not higher. You fall at your worst grip, so predictable friction beats peak friction.
