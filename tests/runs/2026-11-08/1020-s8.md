**The idea behind the Swift 6 concurrency errors you're seeing came from Einstein.**

Lamport's 1978 "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" took relativity's lack of a universal "now" and turned causal ordering into **happens-before**: two events with no message path between them are concurrent, and "which came first" is meaningless. It became one of the most cited CS papers; Lamport has said the part he thought mattered most (replicated state machines) went largely unnoticed.

Twist: Swift 6's strict concurrency checker applies that rule at compile time. Each isolation domain is its own observer; a value crosses only on a happens-before edge (`await`, `Sendable` handoff). "Non-Sendable type crossing actor boundary" is the compiler saying the two places share no "now".

1) Deeper: the bug that led Lamport to the idea, and how vector clocks fixed what his clocks couldn't
2) Sideways: actor reentrancy, the race Swift 6 still won't catch
3) Wildcard: why FT8 stops decoding when your PC clock drifts, the same problem over the air

**Meanwhile:**
**Almost none of the DT column on your FT8 decodes is propagation delay. It's clock error.**

A long-path signal (~40,000 km) arrives in ~0.13 s; a normal 20m DX path takes tens of ms. DT +0.8 is mostly the two stations' clock error plus audio/software lag. FT8 is 79 symbols × 0.16 s = 12.64 s in fixed 15 s slots, with three 7×7 Costas sync arrays (start, middle, end); the decoder slides them in time and frequency. Usual guidance: keep your clock within about a second.

Twist: FT8 is synchronous (shared clock via NTP/GPS, bounded delay → a simple schedule); Swift actors are asynchronous (no bound, no shared clock → only causal order at every `await`). Moonbounce shows the limit: ~2.5 s Earth–Moon round trip breaks FT8's timing assumption, a big reason EME uses slower modes (JT65 historically, Q65 now).

1) Deeper: how three 7×7 Costas arrays let a decoder pull a signal out at about −20 dB SNR
2) Sideways: actor reentrancy, the "probably finishes first" bug in code
3) Wildcard: the 1859 Carrington storm, when telegraph operators sent messages with batteries disconnected

**The idea worth keeping:** a system without a shared clock has two options. Assume a maximum delay and share a schedule, which is how FT8 works, or rely only on causality, which is how Swift actors work. Bugs show up when code silently assumes the first option while running under the second.

**For your migration:** Swift 6 now proves your app has no data races. It does not prove there are no ordering bugs. Actors are reentrant at every `await`, so a check, then an `await`, then an action is still a race that compiles clean and passes tests. Before you merge, a quick scan of the diff for state read before an `await` and used after it is worth five minutes.
