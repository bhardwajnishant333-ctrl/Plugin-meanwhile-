**Meanwhile:**
**Your ViT can find the corn, but it can't tell how much grain is in the ears.** Area estimation and yield estimation are hard in different ways.

**Area is a classification problem.** Each pixel gets a label, and that label stays stable for months. Your model can see the field through spring, summer and harvest. Misclassified pixels at field edges tend to partly cancel out when you add up a county.

**Yield is a regression on hidden variables**, and there are four main reasons it's harder:
- **The key window is short.** For corn, a lot of the final yield is decided during roughly two weeks of pollination in July. Heat or drought in that window can cut yield sharply while the canopy still looks green.
- **The signal saturates.** NDVI flattens out once the canopy is dense (leaf area index around 3 and above). A great field and a merely good one can look the same.
- **Clouds block optical imagery.** Midsummer storms can hide fields from Sentinel-2 during exactly that critical window. That's why people add Sentinel-1 radar, which sees through cloud.
- **Labels are coarse.** In the US, reliable yield ground truth is mostly NASS county averages, not data per field. You end up training a pixel-level model on county-level labels.

**The twist:** errors in area estimates are mostly independent of each other, so they shrink when you aggregate. Yield errors are correlated, because the same heat wave hits every field in a region. Adding more pixels doesn't average them away. That's also why the market moves more on yield than on acreage.
