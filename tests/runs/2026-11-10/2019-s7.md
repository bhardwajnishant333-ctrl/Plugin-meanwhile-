**If a Sidekiq waitlist job keeps failing, it can still be retrying about three weeks after the class it was for.**

**How it works:** By default Sidekiq retries a failed job 25 times. Each wait is roughly `retry_count**4 + 15` seconds, plus a little random jitter. The early waits are short: about 15 seconds, then 16, then 31. Because the count is raised to the fourth power, the later waits are huge. Retry #24 alone waits more than 3.8 days. All the waits add up to about 1.76 million seconds, or roughly 20 days, and Sidekiq's docs say "approximately 21 days." Separately, Sidekiq runs each job at least once, not exactly once. If a process crashes or redeploys mid-job, the job can run twice.

**The twist:** A "promote the next person off the waitlist" job can fail on Thursday, say because of a database timeout. It can then succeed on retry #19 nine days later. At that point it promotes someone into a 6pm Saturday class that already happened, and maybe charges them. Or, because of at-least-once delivery, it gives one open seat to two people. The fix isn't more careful enqueuing. The job should treat its arguments as a hint and re-check everything when it runs: has the class started, is the seat still open, is this person still waitlisted. It should do those checks under a row lock, or with a unique constraint on the booking. You could also set `sidekiq_options retry: 5` or so on time-sensitive jobs, so they fail loudly instead of coming back weeks later.

**Meanwhile:**
**NES Tetris's randomizer makes long-bar droughts shorter than pure chance would, not longer.**

**How it works:** This is the idealized model. The real one uses a 16-bit LFSR plus a counter, and meatfighter's teardown "Applying Artificial Intelligence to Nintendo Tetris" shows the real odds are slightly uneven. The game rolls a number from 0 to 7, which gives 8 outcomes for 7 pieces. If the roll lands on the eighth value, or matches the piece you just got, it rolls again, but only once. That second roll is a value from 0 to 6, and the game accepts it even if it's a repeat.
- **Repeats:** 2/8 × 1/7 = **1/28, about 3.6%**, versus 1/7 (14.3%) for a fair die.
- **Any other specific piece, including the I-piece after a non-I:** 1/8 + 1/28 = **9/56, about 16.1%**, versus 14.3%.
- **Gap between long bars:** about 6.2 pieces on average, versus 7 for a fair 1-in-7 draw.
- **Chance of 12+ pieces with no I-piece:** about 0.839^12 ≈ **12%**, versus about 16% for a fair draw.

**The twist:** droughts feel worse than they are because you can see them, not because they're more common. On a tall stack at level 19 or 29, one 15-piece gap ends the run, and a ~12% chance comes up several times per game. Also notice that the reroll is a single check with no guarantee. It makes repeats about 4 times rarer, but repeats still happen. Your waitlist job has the same shape: checking "is the seat still free?" once, without a lock, makes double-booking rarer but doesn't prevent it. Only a lock or a unique index makes it impossible.
