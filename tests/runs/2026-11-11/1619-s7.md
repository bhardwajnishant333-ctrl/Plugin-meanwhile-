**A navigator takes three star sights and draws the small triangle where the lines cross, called the "cocked hat." With purely random errors, the boat is inside that triangle only about 1 time in 4.**

Here's why. Each line of position has some error, and the error is equally likely to push the line toward the boat or away from it. Three lines give 2³ = 8 equally likely combinations of which side each one fell on. Only 2 of those 8 leave the true position inside the triangle. That works out to 25%, and it doesn't matter how carefully you worked the sights. H. E. Daniels published the result in 1951.

The twist is that the triangle's size tells you almost nothing about how accurate the fix is. A tiny, tidy cocked hat can sit well away from the boat, and a big ugly one can contain it. A systematic error, like an uncorrected index error, makes this worse. It shifts all three lines the same way, and whether the boat lands inside or outside depends on how the stars are spread around the horizon. Tight agreement between your measurements is not the same thing as being right.

**Choose your stars well and the cocked hat stops being a mistake. It becomes a measurement of your mistake.**

Here's the mechanism. A constant error, such as an uncorrected index error, a wrong height of eye for dip, or your personal habit of bringing the star down a touch late, moves every line of position by the same distance. If the error makes your altitudes read high, every line moves toward its star. If it makes them read low, every line moves away. Only three kinds of point sit the same distance from three lines: the centre of the triangle's inscribed circle, and three centres of circles drawn outside it. Which one is your true position depends on where the stars are:

- **Stars spread more than 180° around the horizon** (three stars about 120° apart is the classic choice): you're at the **inside** centre. Bisect the triangle's three angles and the bisectors meet at your position.
- **All stars within 180° of each other** (one side of the sky): you're at one of the **outside** centres, so you're off the triangle. Bisect the angles on the outside instead.

The twist is that in the 120° case, the radius of the inscribed circle roughly equals the constant error itself. A big, neat cocked hat from well-spread stars doesn't mean you took sloppy sights. It means something consistent is off, and the triangle has just told you how much. Real fixes mix constant and random error, so treat that radius as an estimate, not an exact figure. Bowditch's advice to spread your bodies evenly around the horizon is mostly about this: an even spread lets a constant error cancel itself out instead of quietly moving your fix.

1. **Deeper:** four stars 90° apart, and how opposite pairs let you calculate your personal error and remove it.
2. **Sideways:** race handicaps. Time-on-time and time-on-distance scoring can crown different winners from the same finish times.
3. **Wildcard:** why the first transatlantic telegraph cable went dead within weeks of opening in 1858.

**What's wrong:** `finished_at` is declared as `virtual: true` in `Regatta.Results.Finish`. Ecto never stores a virtual field in the database, so a query can't `select` it. The original code was almost certainly loading the whole `Finish` struct and filling in `finished_at` in Elixir afterwards. The rewrite switched to a map `select`, which reaches into the database for that field, so it breaks.

**Fix:** you have two options, depending on where `finished_at` comes from.

**A. It's computed from real columns** (for example, race start plus elapsed seconds). Select those columns and work out `finished_at` in Elixir:

```elixir
def list_finishes(race_id) do
  from(f in Finish,
    join: b in assoc(f, :boat),
    where: f.race_id == ^race_id,
    order_by: [asc: f.corrected_time],
    select: %{boat: b.name, finish: f, corrected: f.corrected_time}
  )
  |> Repo.all()
  |> Enum.map(fn %{finish: f} = row ->
    row
    |> Map.put(:finished_at, Finish.finished_at(f))  # whatever fills the virtual field today
    |> Map.delete(:finish)
  end)
end
```

**B. It's just a renamed or derived copy of a real column** (for example, `finish_time`). Select that column directly: `finished_at: f.finish_time`.

Look in the git diff for the pre-rewrite `list_finishes/1`, or for wherever `finished_at` gets assigned (`grep -rn "finished_at" lib/`). Whatever did that job is what `Finish.finished_at(f)` should call in option A.

Also check that `corrected_time` is a real column. If it's virtual too, the `order_by` and its `select` will fail the same way once `finished_at` is fixed.
