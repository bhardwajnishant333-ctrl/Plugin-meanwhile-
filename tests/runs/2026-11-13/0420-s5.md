**Scala 3 can read your 2.13 jars as-is. Scala 2.13 can only read Scala 3 jars with a flag. Neither can run the other's macros.**

Scala 2 keeps its type information in "pickles." These are bytes stuffed into a `ScalaSignature` annotation inside the `.class` file. Scala 3 has its own reader for that format built in, so `"org" %% "lib_2.13"` works from Scala 3 with no extra setup. That's partly why Scala 3 could ship on the 2.13 standard library at launch. Going the other way, Scala 3 writes `.tasty` files (Typed Abstract Syntax Trees). Scala 2.13 got an experimental TASTy reader around 2.13.4/2.13.5, which you turn on with `-Ytasty-reader`. It can use Scala 3 libraries that don't use Scala 3-only features.

The twist is in what TASTy stores. Scala 2 pickles hold only signatures. TASTy holds full typed trees, including method bodies. That's what lets Scala 3 `inline` reach across library boundaries, since the body is right there to paste in. Macros are a different problem. A macro is code that runs inside the compiler against the compiler's internal API, and the two compilers' internal APIs don't share anything. So a 2.13 dependency that relies on Scala 2 macros, like shapeless 2, older circe-generic derivation, or some logging and JSON codecs, can't expand its macros under Scala 3. It needs a version built for Scala 3. So "the jars load" and "the project compiles" are two different claims. Usually the long pole is the macro-heavy dependencies, not your code.

**Mars Climate Orbiter didn't fail from one big error. It failed from a small error repeated many times.**

Lockheed Martin's ground software, `SM_FORCES`, reported thruster impulse in pound-force seconds. JPL's navigation software read those numbers as newton-seconds, which the interface spec actually required. That's a factor of about 4.45. Each burn was tiny, though. They were "angular momentum desaturation" firings that kept the reaction wheels from spinning too fast. The spacecraft had a single, lopsided solar array, so sunlight pushed on it unevenly and it needed these firings far more often than a balanced design would. Over a 9-month cruise, many small, mislabeled nudges added up. On September 23, 1999, it reached Mars at about 57 km altitude instead of the planned ~140-150 km, and it either burned up or skipped off the atmosphere.

The twist: people did notice. Navigators saw the trajectory drifting in ways their models didn't explain and raised it informally. But it never went through the formal anomaly process, and nobody traced it back to the units. The mishap board blamed process failures as much as the conversion. The bug was a missing type. The loss was a missing escalation.

For a payments service in Scala 3, this has a direct fix: `opaque type Cents = Long`. It costs nothing at runtime, and the compiler stops you from mixing it with `Pence` or plain `Long`. Your migration is a cheap time to add it.

**In 1983, a Boeing 767 ran out of fuel at 41,000 feet even though its fuel had been checked by hand several times.**

Air Canada Flight 143, July 23, 1983, Montreal to Edmonton. The 767 was among the first metric aircraft in a fleet that still thought in pounds. Its fuel gauges were dead from a faulty processor, so the crew measured the tanks with dipsticks and converted litres to mass by hand. The trip needed 22,300 kg. They used 1.77, which is pounds per litre, where they needed about 0.8 kilograms per litre. So they loaded about 22,300 *pounds*, a bit over 10,000 kg, which is less than half the fuel they needed. Over Red Lake, Ontario, both engines quit. Captain Bob Pearson happened to be an experienced glider pilot. First Officer Maurice Quintal had served at RCAF Station Gimli, a closed air force base whose runway was by then used for drag racing and was hosting a family event that day. Pearson side-slipped the 767 like a glider to lose height, and the nose gear collapsed on landing. All 69 people on board survived, with minor injuries from the evacuation.

The twist: the arithmetic was done more than once, by ground staff and by the crew. Every check reused the same 1.77.
