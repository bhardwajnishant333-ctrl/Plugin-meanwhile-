**Your sax is a cone pretending to be a pipe, and the mouthpiece is standing in for the missing tip of the cone.**

A cylinder closed at one end, like a clarinet, produces mostly odd harmonics. Its register key jumps you up a twelfth, so clarinetists have to learn a second set of fingerings for the upper register. A cone closed at its point behaves differently. Acoustically it acts like a pipe open at both ends: it produces the full harmonic series and overblows at the octave. That's why your fingerings repeat an octave up and you only press the octave key.

A sax isn't a complete cone, though. Adolphe Sax (patented 1846) cut off the point, and you can't blow into a point anyway. The mouthpiece fills in for the missing piece. Its internal volume has to roughly equal the volume of the cone that was cut away, or the instrument stops behaving like a cone.

The part that applies to you: moving the mouthpiece in or out on the cork doesn't just shift the whole horn sharp or flat. It changes how well the mouthpiece matches the missing cone. So if you tune your low notes by sliding the mouthpiece, your octaves can drift wide or narrow. That may be part of why your upper register keeps disagreeing with the piano, and some of it may not be you. (The principle is solid, but how much it matters depends on your horn and mouthpiece.)

**"Never use your database as a queue" was solid advice for about 20 years. Then one SQL clause took away most of the reason for it.**

A database queue used to fail like this. Every worker ran the same query: `SELECT ... ORDER BY id LIMIT 1 FOR UPDATE`. They all reached for the oldest row at the same moment. One got the lock and the rest sat waiting on it. Ten workers ended up working one at a time, which is why Redis-backed tools like Resque and then Sidekiq took over.

The fix was `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. Postgres added it in 9.5 (2016) and MySQL in 8.0 (2018). With it, a worker that finds a row already locked moves on to the next free one instead of waiting. Workers stop queuing behind each other, and Postgres can serve as a reasonable job queue. Solid Queue is built on that clause, and it's the reason it needs those database versions or newer. 37signals runs HEY on it at millions of jobs a day. I've seen about 20M a day cited, but I'm not certain of that number.

**The twist, and it matters for your migration:** you can now get something Sidekiq can't give you. If the job row lives in the same database as your data, enqueuing the job is part of the same transaction. Sidekiq has a familiar race: the job runs before the record's transaction commits, and it fails with "record not found." Rails 7.2 added `enqueue_after_transaction_commit` largely to work around that. But Rails 8's default setup puts Solid Queue in a *separate* `queue` database, so you don't get the atomic enqueue automatically. It's worth checking which setup Claude is generating in `config/database.yml`.

**US time zones weren't created by the government. Railroads set them, and Congress made them law 35 years later.**

Before 1883, every town kept its own local solar time, with noon set by when the sun was highest. Railroads each kept their own schedule time too, and there were dozens of them. A station could have several clocks on the wall, each showing a different railroad's time. Getting a connection meant converting between railroads' clocks.

On November 18, 1883, the railroads switched to four standard zones by agreement among themselves. It became known as "the day of two noons," because towns on the eastern side of each zone had to set their clocks back and hit noon a second time. There was no law behind it. Some cities refused; Detroit kept its own time for years. The Standard Time Act, which gave the zones legal force, wasn't passed until 1918.

**The twist:** the people who needed shared time most made it real by just using it. The law came afterwards and mostly confirmed what was already in place. The same pattern played out with TCP/IP, JSON, and arguably Rails defaults like "convention over configuration": a de facto standard wins first and gets formalized later.
