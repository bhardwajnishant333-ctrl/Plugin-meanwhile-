**Your swarm picks its new home without any bee ever comparing two sites.**

When a colony swarms, a few hundred scouts go out looking for cavities. A scout that finds a good one comes back and waggle-dances for it. The better the site, the longer she dances. Each time she flies back to check it, she dances fewer waggle runs, roughly 15 fewer per trip in Thomas Seeley's counts, until she stops completely. So no single bee's support lasts forever.

Two more mechanisms finish the job:
- **Cross-inhibition:** in 2012, Seeley's group showed that scouts head-butt dancers who are promoting a rival site and give a short vibration (the "stop signal"). This breaks ties between sites that are about equally good.
- **Quorum, not consensus:** scouts at the site keep count of each other. When about 15 to 20 are there at the same time, they go back and start "piping," which tells the cluster to warm their flight muscles to about 35°C. Then the whole swarm leaves. Most bees never voted.

**The twist:** the colony doesn't wait for agreement. It uses support that runs out unless it's renewed, plus a threshold. A bad site loses because its supporters stop dancing, not because anyone proves it's worse. That's a lease with a heartbeat. It's the same pattern behind the activity heartbeats and timeouts Claude is wiring into your Temporal workflows right now, and the bees got there millions of years earlier.
